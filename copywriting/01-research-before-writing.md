# Rule 01: Research before you write

## What it is

Collect your customers' own words before you write a single line. Reviews, surveys, support tickets, sales calls and interviews tell you what people want, what worries them and the exact phrases they use. The copy then repeats those phrases back.

## Why it works

Readers trust copy that describes their problem the way they would describe it. A writer working from imagination produces generic claims. A writer working from research produces lines a customer recognises.

The clearest worked example is Conversion Rate Experts' project for Crazy Egg. The team ran on-page surveys at checkout to capture visitors' questions, reused support staff explanations as copy, and ran a customer case-study contest to collect testimonials in real customer language. The rebuilt page, organised around those questions, beat the original by 30%. The rebuilt checkout copy produced 116% more sign-ups.

The Moz project worked the same way: Conversion Rate Experts built the page from the CEO's live sales pitch. The rule they took from it: if a good salesperson answers a question on a call, the page should answer it too.

## How to do it

Review mining (Copyhackers method). Search Google for reviews of your category and its alternatives:

```
site:amazon.com inurl:"product-reviews" "tired of" <your keyword>
```

Repeat with phrases such as "frustrated by", "wish it" and "finally". From each review, pull out:

- problems in the customer's life
- problems with their current solution
- what they want instead
- favourite features
- hesitations before buying

Record how often each item comes up and copy the exact wording into a spreadsheet.

Other free sources: your own support inbox, sales call notes, a one-question survey on the pricing or checkout page ("What's stopping you from signing up today?"), Google reviews of competitors, Reddit threads in your niche.

## Bad

> Our platform delivers powerful insights that help teams work smarter.

Written from a desk. No customer said "powerful insights".

## Good

> Stop guessing why people leave your pricing page. See where they click and where they give up.

Built from review phrases like "I had no idea why people weren't buying" and "I was guessing".

## Fix

Before drafting, fill a sheet with at least 20 real customer phrases grouped by pain, desire, hesitation and favourite feature. Write the headline from the most frequent pain. Answer the top three hesitations on the page. If you can't point to the source of a phrase, you made it up.

Evidence: consultant case studies with no sample sizes published. The method is standard practice among conversion copywriters.
