# Rule 02: Settle positioning before the headline

## What it is

Positioning is the decision about what you are, who you are for and why you beat the alternatives. Messaging is how you say it. Positioning comes first.

April Dunford's positioning work breaks it into parts:

- the real alternatives your customer would use if you didn't exist (often a spreadsheet, an agency or doing nothing)
- what you do differently from those alternatives
- the value that difference creates
- the customers who care most about that value
- the market category that makes the value obvious

## Why it matters

When a headline feels impossible to write, the problem is usually upstream. The writer doesn't know which alternative they are beating or who the page is for, so every draft comes out vague. Copy can't fix a positioning gap. It can only hide it behind adjectives.

## Check

Fill in this sentence without adjectives:

> For [specific customer] who currently use [real alternative], we [do this differently], so they get [measurable value].

If you can't fill it, stop writing copy and go back to customer research (Rule 01).

## Bad

> The all-in-one solution for modern businesses.

No customer, no alternative, no difference. Every competitor could print this.

## Good

> Booking and payments for dental clinics in the Gulf. Replaces the phone diary and the card machine with one link patients can use in Arabic or English.

Customer (dental clinics in the Gulf), alternative (phone diary, card machine), difference (one bilingual link).

## Fix

Write the positioning sentence first and keep it at the top of your draft. Every headline option must be a way of saying that sentence. Delete any option that says something else.

Evidence: convention. Widely taught in B2B marketing; no controlled test.
