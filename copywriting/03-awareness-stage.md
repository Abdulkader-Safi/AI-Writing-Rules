# Rule 03: Open where the reader's awareness is

## What it is

Eugene Schwartz's five stages of awareness, from _Breakthrough Advertising_, describe how much a reader already knows. The stage decides how the page should open and how long it needs to be.

| Stage          | What the reader knows            | How the copy opens                               | Typical traffic                                 |
| -------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Most aware     | Knows you and wants it           | The offer and price, then how to buy             | Branded search, retargeting, existing customers |
| Product aware  | Knows you, unsure you fit        | How you differ from alternatives                 | Comparison searches, review sites               |
| Solution aware | Knows solutions exist, not yours | Proof yours fits best                            | Category searches                               |
| Problem aware  | Feels the pain, knows no fix     | Name the pain in their words                     | Problem searches, social ads                    |
| Unaware        | Doesn't know there's a problem   | A story or shared frustration, no pitch up front | Cold social, content                            |

## Why it works

A most-aware visitor who lands on a story about their problem gets bored and leaves. A problem-aware visitor who lands on a price table has no reason to care yet. Matching the opening to the reader removes the mismatch. Wrong stage, lost reader.

It also explains the long-versus-short debate. Most-aware visitors need a short page. Problem-aware and unaware visitors bring more questions, so they need more copy.

## Check

For each page, write down where most of its traffic comes from. Look that source up in the table. Does the first screen open the way the table says? If not, rewrite it.

## Bad

A Google Ads campaign on the search "how to stop invoice late payments" (problem aware) lands on:

> Plans from $19/month. Start your free trial.

## Good

> Clients paying you 30, 60, 90 days late? Most never meant to. They forgot. Paydue sends polite reminders for you, so you stop chasing.

The same product, for branded search traffic (most aware):

> Paydue plans from $19/month. 14-day free trial, no card.

## Fix

Split pages by traffic source when the stages differ a lot. A homepage usually serves product-aware and most-aware visitors. Ads and content pages often need their own landing pages written for problem-aware readers.

Source: [Adbeat: the 5 levels of customer awareness](https://blog.adbeat.com/5-levels-customer-awareness/)

Evidence: convention, from a classic advertising text. Adbeat dates the book to 1952; the commonly cited first edition is 1966. No controlled test of the model itself.
