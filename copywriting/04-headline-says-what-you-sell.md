# Rule 04: The headline says what you sell

## What it is

The main headline (the "hero header") must tell a visitor exactly what you offer. A slogan, a mood or a promise with no subject fails.

## Why it works

Visitors decide in seconds whether to stay. NN/g's eye-tracking found 57% of viewing time goes to the first screen of a page and 74% to the first two screens. If the first line doesn't say what this is, many visitors never find out.

Julian Shapiro's test: a visitor who reads only the header should know exactly what you sell. NN/g gives the same rule for homepages: replace "Welcome to..." with a plain statement of what you offer, and skip jargon.

Real test result (Moz, 2010): the old headline "Improve your traffic and rankings with an SEOmoz PRO Membership!" was replaced with "When eBay, Disney and Marriott need SEO help, here's what they do..." as part of a page rebuild that lifted sales 52% across 5,000+ visitors. The page changed many things at once, so the headline alone can't take the credit.

Real test result (Highrise, 2009): a headline leading with the 30-day free trial, with "less than 60 seconds" in the subhead, beat "Start a Highrise Account" by 30% in clicks on Sign Up, across 4,000 page views.

## Check

Show the headline alone to someone outside your business for five seconds. Ask: "What does this company sell?" If they can't answer, rewrite it.

## Bad

> Improve your workflow!

> The future of payroll is here.

> Welcome to Brightline. Powering your digital journey.

## Good

> Your real estate leads get a text within a minute, and the qualified ones land in your calendar.

> Payroll and tax filing for US teams of 1 to 50.

> Brightline builds booking and payment systems for clinics in Australia and the Gulf.

## Fix

Start the headline with the thing itself: the product, the service or the result. Add who it's for if the market is narrow. Cut "welcome", "the future of", "next-generation" and any line that would still make sense on a competitor's site.
