# Rule 06: Write for the scan

## What it is

Most visitors don't read a web page. They scan it. Put the important words where scanning eyes land: the first two paragraphs, the start of each heading and the start of each paragraph.

## Why it works

NN/g's analysis of 45,237 page views found users have time to read at most 28% of the words on an average page, and 20% is more likely. Each extra 100 words bought about 4.4 more seconds of attention. That data is old (collected 2005, published 2008) and skewed toward skilled users, so a broad audience reads even less.

NN/g's eye-tracking found the F-shaped scanning pattern in 2006 and confirmed it on desktop and mobile in 2017. Arabic and other right-to-left readers show a mirrored F. Other patterns include the layer-cake, where eyes jump from heading to heading, and the spotted pattern, where people hunt for numbers or specific words.

The inverted pyramid, the journalism rule of putting the conclusion first, follows from this: readers who stop early still get the point.

## Check

- Read only the headings on the page. Do they tell the story by themselves?
- Does each heading start with its most informative word?
- Does the first sentence under each heading answer that heading?
- Are numbers written as digits ("3 days", not "three days") so spotted scanning finds them?

## Bad

> ## Our approach
>
> At our company, we have always believed that the best way to approach a project is to start by understanding the client's needs, which is why our first step is always a discovery call...

The heading says nothing. The point arrives in line three.

## Good

> ## Free 30-minute call before any quote
>
> We start with a call to understand what you need. You get a fixed quote within 2 days.

## Fix

Rewrite each heading so it works as a summary. Move the conclusion of each section into its first sentence. Use digits. Break any paragraph over about five sentences. Cut what a scanner would skip anyway.

Source: [NN/g: how little do users read (2008)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/), [NN/g: F-shaped pattern (2017)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/), [NN/g: inverted pyramid (2018)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/inverted-pyramid/)

Evidence: strong (independent eye-tracking and usability research), though the reading-time data is old.
