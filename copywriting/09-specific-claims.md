# Rule 09: Make every claim specific

## What it is

A specific claim names a number, a time, a person or a thing you can picture. A vague claim could be said by anyone and proved by no one.

Harry Dry of Marketing Examples tests each line of copy with three questions:

- Can you visualise it?
- Can you falsify it (could it be proved wrong)?
- Can nobody else say it?

## Why it works

Specific claims are believable because they can be checked. Vague claims trigger the reader's "marketing" filter and get skipped.

- "1,000 songs in your pocket" passes the first test. "32GB storage" fails it.
- "Best service in town" fails the second test. Nobody can prove it false, so nobody believes it.
- A line your competitor could paste onto their site fails the third.

The test evidence points the same way. The winning Highrise headline led with a specific offer (a 30-day free trial, signup in under 60 seconds). The winning Moz headline named eBay, Disney and Marriott.

## Check

Run every headline and claim through the three questions. Any line that fails two of them gets rewritten or deleted.

## Bad

> Fast, reliable hosting.

> We deliver exceptional results for our clients.

> Trusted by businesses everywhere.

## Good

> Your site loads in under a second from Sydney and Dubai.

> We moved Bumper to Bumper's 16 branches onto one booking system in 6 weeks.

> Used by 412 clinics in Kuwait and Saudi Arabia.

(Examples are illustrative. Use only figures you can prove.)

## Fix

For each vague word, ask "how much, how fast, who, compared to what?" Write the answer instead. If you don't have the answer, go find it (customer data, case studies, analytics) before publishing the claim.
