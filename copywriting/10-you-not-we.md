# Rule 10: Write about the reader, not about yourself

## What it is

Most first drafts talk about the company: "We are", "Our team", "We believe". The reader came to find out what's in it for them. Write about their problem and their result. Then prove it.

## Why it works

Visitors scan for the answer to their own question (Rule 06). Paragraphs that open with "we" push that answer further down. NN/g's About Us research found that even on About pages, users want to know what the company does and whether to trust it, not its self-description.

No study was found that compares "you"-focused and "we"-focused copy directly. This rule is convention, supported by the scanning and plain-language research.

## Check

Count sentences that start with "We", "Our" or the company name. If they outnumber sentences that start with "You" or the reader's situation, rewrite.

## Bad

> We are a passionate team of developers who have been building websites since 2012. We pride ourselves on quality, and our process is designed around our values.

## Good

> You get a website that loads fast, ranks on Google and is easy for your staff to update. We've built 140 of them since 2012.

"We" still appears, but as proof for the reader's benefit.

## Fix

Rewrite each "we" sentence from the reader's side: what do they get, avoid or feel? Keep "we" for facts that prove the claim (experience, numbers, guarantees).

Source: [NN/g: About Us information on websites (2019)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/)

Evidence: convention. No direct test found.
