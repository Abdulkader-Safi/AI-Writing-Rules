# Rule 11: Keep one voice, change the tone

## What it is

Voice is the brand's personality. It stays the same on every page. Tone is how that voice adjusts to the reader's situation. A worried reader on an error page gets a calmer, plainer tone than a curious reader on the homepage.

## Why it works

Mailchimp's content style guide is the standard model. Its voice "doesn't change much from day to day", while tone shifts with the reader's mood. Its voice traits (plainspoken, genuine, a translator of jargon, dry humour) all sit under one rule: clarity beats entertainment. "If you're unsure, keep a straight face."

Consistent voice makes a site feel like one company. Matching tone to the moment avoids jokes on a payment-failed screen.

No research was found linking voice consistency to business results, so treat this as good practice, not a proven lever.

## How to write a voice document

Pick three or four traits. For each, write:

- "We are X, not Y" (for example, "Direct, not blunt")
- one example sentence in that voice
- how far it flexes between the homepage and an error message

Keep the whole document to one page so writers actually use it.

## Bad

Homepage:

> Let's gooo! Your invoices are about to get SO much easier!

Payment failed screen:

> Oops! Looks like something went wrong lol.

## Good

Homepage:

> Send an invoice in two minutes. Get paid in days, not weeks.

Payment failed screen:

> Your card was declined, so no payment was taken. Check the card details or try another card.

## Fix

Write the voice traits down. Before publishing a page, ask what state the reader is in (curious, comparing, worried, annoyed) and set the tone to match. When in doubt, plainer.

Source: [Mailchimp content style guide: voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)
