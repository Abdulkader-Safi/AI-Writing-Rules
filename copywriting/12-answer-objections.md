# Rule 12: Answer every objection on the page

## What it is

An objection is a doubt that stops someone acting: "Is it too expensive?", "Will it work for me?", "What's the catch?", "How hard is it to set up?" Conversion copy lists these doubts and answers each one, with proof, near the point where it comes up.

## Why it works

The best-documented conversion gains come from removing a specific doubt right where someone is about to act. Conversion Rate Experts call it unblocking arteries: visitors who are ready but hesitant need specific doubts removed.

At Crazy Egg, each objection got its own answer: what heatmaps are worth, the price, confusion with Google Analytics, and having fewer features than competitors. The price objection was answered with a third-party research figure set against the cost of eye-tracking studies. The new checkout copy explained that the free trial costs nothing, why a card is needed and how yearly billing works. Sign-ups rose 116%.

When Moz offered a $1 trial without explaining it, about 500 people wrote in asking whether there was a catch. Unexplained offers create suspicion.

## How to find the objections

- Ask sales staff which questions come up on every call
- Read support tickets from people who almost didn't buy
- Run a one-question survey on the pricing or checkout page: "What's stopping you today?"
- Mine the "hesitations" column from review research (Rule 01)

## Bad

A pricing page with plans and a "Start trial" button, and nothing about the card field that appears next.

## Good

Under the button:

> 14-day free trial. We ask for a card so your account keeps working if you decide to stay. You won't be charged until day 15, and you can cancel from your dashboard in one click.

## Fix

Make an objection list before writing. For each item, write a one-line answer and attach proof (a number, a testimonial, a guarantee, a screenshot). Place each answer next to the part of the page where the doubt arises: price doubts at pricing, setup doubts near "how it works", trust doubts near the CTA.

Source: [Conversion Rate Experts: Crazy Egg case study](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/crazy-egg-case-study/), [Conversion Rate Experts: Moz case study](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/moz-case-study/)

Evidence: consultant case studies, no sample sizes published. The most consistent finding across the copy tests in this research.
