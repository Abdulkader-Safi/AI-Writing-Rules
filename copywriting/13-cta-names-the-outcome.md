# Rule 13: The button says what happens next

## What it is

A call to action (CTA) is the button or link that asks for the action. Its label should name what the visitor gets or what happens when they click. "Submit", "Click here" and "Learn more" name nothing.

## Why it works

The button is the last thing read before the decision. A label that names the outcome reminds the visitor why they're clicking and lowers the sense of risk. "Submit" wastes that moment.

Test results, all old or vendor-published, with no sample sizes reported:

| Change                                           | Result                       | Source                          |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| "Start your free trial" to "Start my free trial" | +90% sign-ups                | ContentVerve, 2013              |
| "Request a quote" to "Request pricing"           | click-through 0.54% to 1.40% | Veeam via VWO, clicks not leads |

Treat these as ideas to test, not numbers to expect. The first-person "my" result has been repeated on other sites with smaller lifts.

Julian Shapiro's guidance: the CTA should say what the visitor gets, and buttons should be a single action word or short verb phrase.

## Check

- Does the label start with a verb?
- Does it name the thing they get?
- Would it still make sense if it were the only text on the page?

## Bad

> Submit

> Click here

> Learn more

> Get started (on a page where "started" with what is unclear)

## Good

> Get my quote

> Start free trial

> Download the 2026 salary report

> Book a 20-minute call

## Fix

Finish the sentence "I want to..." from the visitor's side and use the end of it as the label. Keep it to two to five words. On a page with several buttons, make the primary one the same wording each time it repeats.

Source: [MarketingExperiments on ContentVerve's CTA test (2013)](https://marketingexperiments.com/?p=12262), [VWO: Veeam case study](https://wingify.com/success-stories/veeam-software/), [Julian Shapiro: landing pages](https://www.julian.com/guide/growth/landing-pages)

Evidence: anecdote (single tests, no sample sizes), plus practitioner guidance.
