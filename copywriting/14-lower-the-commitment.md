# Rule 14: Spell out the terms next to the button

## What it is

Right under or beside the CTA, add a short line that removes the last doubt: what it costs, whether a card is needed, how to cancel, what the guarantee is. This is often called risk reversal. Every line must be true.

## Why it works

People hesitate at the moment of commitment. A line like "No card required" or "Cancel any time" answers the question they're about to ask.

Evidence:

- Crazy Egg (Conversion Rate Experts): checkout copy explaining the free trial, the card request and yearly billing produced 116% more sign-ups.
- Highrise (2009): the subhead "pay as you go, no long-term contracts, no hidden fees, no surprises" lifted Sign Up clicks 27%.
- Moz: a $1 trial with no explanation drew about 500 messages asking about the catch.

## What to state

| If your offer has... | Say it near the CTA                                               |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A free trial         | How long, whether a card is needed, when the first charge happens |
| A subscription       | Monthly or yearly, how to cancel                                  |
| A guarantee          | The exact terms ("Full refund within 30 days, no questions")      |
| A free consultation  | How long, what happens after, no obligation                       |
| A form               | Who replies, how fast                                             |

## Bad

> [Start trial]

(Then a card form appears with no explanation.)

## Good

> [Start free trial]
> 14 days free. No card needed. Cancel from your dashboard any time.

> [Book a call]
> 20 minutes with a senior engineer. You'll get a fixed quote within 2 days, with no obligation.

## Fix

List every term a cautious buyer would ask about. Put the two or three that matter most in one line below the button. Link the rest to an FAQ on the same page. Never claim "no card" or "cancel any time" unless both are literally true. See Rule 18 for the legal side.

Source: [Conversion Rate Experts: Crazy Egg](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/crazy-egg-case-study/), [Signal v. Noise: Highrise headline tests (2009)](https://signalvnoise.com/posts/1525-writing-decisions-headline-tests-on-the-highrise-signup-page), [Conversion Rate Experts: Moz](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/moz-case-study/)

Evidence: consultant case studies and one old, small test. Consistent direction across all of them.
