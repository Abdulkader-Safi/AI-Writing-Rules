# Rule 15: Explain every form field that looks odd

## What it is

Microcopy is the small text around forms: labels, hints, error messages and the line that says why you need something. Forms are where many visitors quit, so this text does a lot of work.

## Why it works

Baymard Institute's checkout research:

- 14% of users will abandon checkout if a phone number is required with no reason given. 39% of e-commerce sites don't explain it.
- 98% of sites don't use error messages that say what specifically is wrong.
- Only 14% of checkouts mark both required and optional fields.

NN/g's form guidance (2016) adds: every field you cut raises conversion, labels belong above fields (not inside as placeholder text that disappears), errors must be specific and visible, and colour alone is not enough to signal an error.

## Rules

- Cut every field you don't strictly need.
- Put a short reason next to any field that looks unnecessary (phone, date of birth, company size).
- Mark required and optional fields clearly. Allow one or two optional fields at most.
- Put labels above fields.
- Give the format before the user types, not after they get it wrong.
- Write errors that say exactly what to fix, and keep what the user already typed.
- One clear primary button. No Reset or Clear button.

## Bad

> Phone number *

> Invalid input.

> Error: field required.

## Good

> Phone number (only used if there's a problem with your delivery)

> Enter your postcode as 4 digits, for example 2000.

> Add your email so we can send your receipt.

## Fix

Go through the form field by field. For each, ask: do we need this now? If yes, would a cautious person wonder why? If yes again, add a one-line reason. Then trigger every error and rewrite each message as an instruction.

Source: [Baymard: checkout form field descriptions](https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-form-field-descriptions), [Baymard: required and optional fields](https://baymard.com/blog/required-optional-form-fields), [NN/g: web form design (2016)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/)

Evidence: strong (independent usability research), NN/g article is from 2016.
