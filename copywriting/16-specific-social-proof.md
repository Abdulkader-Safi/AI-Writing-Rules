# Rule 16: Use specific proof, placed next to the claim

## What it is

Social proof is evidence that other people chose you and it worked: testimonials, reviews, client logos, case-study numbers, customer counts. It works best when it's specific and sits next to the claim it supports.

## Why it works

Specific proof is believable for the same reason specific claims are (Rule 09): it can be checked.

Placement matters. In the WikiJob test (via VWO), testimonials placed low on the page showed no clear effect. Three lines of "sober" testimonials placed near the offer lifted purchases 34%. The case study is undated with no sample size.

NN/g's About Us research found users cross-check on-site testimonials against third-party reviews and grow more sceptical each year. Balanced, honest testimonials beat polished ones. Reviews came up more than 40 times across 20 sessions.

Copyhackers' own sales page uses dated, numbered results such as "I closed a $50K project [Q3, 2024]".

## Check

Each testimonial should have:

- a real name and role (or company)
- a specific result, ideally with a number and a time frame
- a position next to the claim or doubt it answers

## Bad

> "Great product, highly recommend!" - J.

> "Amazing service, five stars."

A wall of 20 testimonials at the bottom of the page.

## Good

> "We cut onboarding from 6 weeks to 9 days in our first quarter." - Priya Shah, Head of People, Northwind

Placed under the claim "Get new staff productive in days".

> "I was worried about moving 3,000 products. They did it over a weekend and nothing broke." - Ahmed, owner, Arta Supplies

Placed next to the migration FAQ.

(Illustrative. Use only real, permissioned quotes.)

## Fix

Sort your testimonials by which objection they answer (price, setup, results, trust). Put each one beside that objection. Ask customers specific questions to get specific quotes: "What changed after you started? How long did it take?" Collect them through a short survey or a case-study contest, as Conversion Rate Experts did for Crazy Egg.

Source: [VWO: WikiJob case study](https://wingify.com/success-stories/wikijob/), [NN/g: About Us information (2019)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/), [Copyhackers sales page (2026)](https://copyhackers.com/ultimate-guide-landing-page-copy)

Evidence: vendor anecdote for the lift; strong for user scepticism (NN/g, 2019).
