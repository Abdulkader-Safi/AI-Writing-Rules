# Rule 17: Never write or "improve" a testimonial

## What it is

Every review, testimonial, customer count and logo on the page must be real, permissioned and on file. The copywriter's job is to find and place proof, not to invent or polish it.

## Why it matters

The US Federal Trade Commission's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials rule (announced August 2024) bans:

- fake reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones and those from people with no real experience of the product
- paying or rewarding people for positive reviews (or conditioning the reward on sentiment)
- undisclosed reviews by staff, managers or their families
- company-controlled "independent" review sites
- suppressing negative reviews
- buying fake followers or views

The FTC can seek civil penalties from knowing violators. Fake proof is now a legal problem.

The rule is US law. Australian and GCC sites that sell into the US should take note. Local consumer law (for example the Australian Consumer Law) was not researched here and should be checked separately.

Apart from the law, NN/g found users check on-site testimonials against outside reviews. A testimonial that sounds too good costs trust.

## Rules

- Use the customer's words. Trim for length only if the meaning stays the same, and get their OK.
- Keep a record: who said it, when, and written permission to use it.
- Disclose any incentive or relationship ("Received a free month for their review").
- Don't show a hand-picked set of reviews as if it were all of them.
- Keep customer counts, logos and "most popular" badges true and current.

## Bad

Writing a testimonial yourself and putting a first name on it.

Editing "It was OK, setup took a while but support helped" into "Setup was easy and support was amazing!"

## Good

> "Setup took a while, but support walked us through it and we've had no problems since." - Laila M., practice manager

The honest version with a mild negative often reads as more believable.

## Fix

Build a proof file: a spreadsheet with each quote, name, date, source, permission and any incentive. Nothing goes on the page unless it's in the file. No record, no quote.

Source: [FTC: final rule banning fake reviews and testimonials (2024)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials), [NN/g: About Us information (2019)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/)

Evidence: strong (official rule text). This is a legal summary, not legal advice.
