# Rule 18: Use only real urgency and state billing plainly

## What it is

Urgency and scarcity ("only 3 left", "offer ends Friday") push people to act now. They are honest only when the deadline or limit is real. Recurring-billing terms belong near the CTA, in plain words.

## Why it matters

Scarcity is one of Robert Cialdini's persuasion principles (with reciprocity, social proof, authority, liking, commitment, and unity added in 2016). These principles only work honestly when the facts behind them are true. Fake urgency is a dark pattern: a design trick that pushes people into something they didn't intend.

On the legal side, the FTC's click-to-cancel rule was struck down by the Eighth Circuit on 8 July 2025, on procedural grounds. Other US law still applies: ROSCA (the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act), Section 5 of the FTC Act and state auto-renewal laws. Those require clear disclosure of recurring charges and a simple way to cancel.

## Honest urgency

- A real deadline ("Early-bird price ends 15 December")
- A real cohort or event date ("Next intake starts 3 March, 20 seats")
- Real stock ("4 left", if the number comes from your inventory)
- A real cost of waiting ("Prices rise in January with the new VAT rate")

## Dishonest urgency

- Countdown timers that reset on every visit
- "Only 2 left" when stock is unlimited
- Pre-ticked add-ons or insurance
- "Someone in Dubai just bought this" pop-ups that aren't real
- Hidden auto-renewal

## Bad

> Hurry! Only 2 spots left! [timer resets to 10:00 on refresh]

> $1 trial (renews at $99/month in small grey text at the bottom)

## Good

> Spring intake starts 3 March. 20 seats, 7 left.

> $1 for your first 7 days, then $99/month. Cancel online any time before day 8 and you won't be charged again.

## Fix

For each urgency claim, ask: could I show a regulator the data behind this? If not, remove it. Put renewal price, billing frequency and how to cancel in the same line as the trial offer.
