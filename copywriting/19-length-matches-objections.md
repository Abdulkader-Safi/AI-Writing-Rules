# Rule 19: Let the number of objections set the length

## What it is

There's no right length for a page. The right length is whatever it takes to answer the reader's real questions, and no more. Cheap, simple offers need short pages. Expensive, complex or unfamiliar offers need longer ones.

## Why it works

The evidence looks contradictory until you see what each source measured.

For long pages:

- Moz (2010): a page six times longer, built from the CEO's sales pitch, lifted sales 52% across 5,000+ visitors.
- Crazy Egg (Conversion Rate Experts): a page about 20 times longer beat the control by 30%. Their line: "you cannot have a page that's too long, only one that's too boring."
- Highrise (2011): a long single-page homepage lifted net signups 37.5% across 42,000+ visitors.

For short pages:

- Unbounce (2024): across 41,000+ landing pages, word count correlated negatively with conversion (-18.6%), as did reading time (-19.4%).

Both can be right. The long-page wins were paid software with real questions to answer. Unbounce's median page is often a simple offer like a free download. And every long-page win changed several things at once, so length alone is unproven.

## Rule of thumb

| Short page                            | Long page                            |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Free or cheap                         | High price                           |
| Low commitment (download, newsletter) | Contract, subscription, big decision |
| Most-aware traffic                    | Cold or problem-aware traffic        |
| Familiar product type                 | New category                         |

## Bad

A 4,000-word page for a free PDF download.

A 150-word page asking for a $20,000 project with no proof, process or FAQ.

## Good

Free checklist: headline, three bullets on what's inside, email field, button.

Agency service page: hero with CTA, the problem, how the work runs, case studies with numbers, pricing guide, FAQ answering the top objections, CTA repeated after each major section.

## Fix

Count the objections on your list (Rule 12). Each one earns a section. When the list runs out, stop writing. On long pages, put the offer and CTA at the top and repeat the CTA after each major section, so ready buyers can act early and doubters can keep reading.

Source: [Conversion Rate Experts: Moz](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/moz-case-study/), [Conversion Rate Experts: Crazy Egg](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/crazy-egg-case-study/), [Signal v. Noise: Highrise long-form test (2011)](https://signalvnoise.com/posts/2977-behind-the-scenes-highrise-marketing-site-ab-testing-part-1), [Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report (2024)](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/?trk=s-bl)

Evidence: old multi-change tests on one side, vendor correlation on the other. The rule of thumb is an inference from both.
