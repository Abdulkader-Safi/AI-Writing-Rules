# Rule 20: Use frameworks as outlines, not formulas

## What it is

Copy frameworks (AIDA, PAS and others) are ways to order an argument. They help once you know what you need to say. They don't tell you what to say, and none has been shown to beat another.

## Why

No head-to-head comparison of frameworks was found. Conversion Rate Experts' winning Crazy Egg page was organised around visitors' objections, not a named formula. Research decides the content (Rule 01); a framework only decides the order.

## The common frameworks

| Framework                        | Order                                                               | Best fit                            |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| AIDA                             | Attention, interest, desire, action                                 | Full landing pages                  |
| PAS                              | Problem, agitate, solution                                          | Problem-aware cold traffic          |
| PASO                             | PAS plus outcome                                                    | Same, with a picture of life after  |
| BAB                              | Before, after, bridge                                               | Transformation offers, testimonials |
| FAB                              | Feature, advantage, benefit                                         | Product and feature pages           |
| StoryBrand                       | Customer as hero, brand as guide, a plan, a CTA, success or failure | Service-business homepages          |
| One reader, one idea, one action | A single person, a single argument, a single ask                    | Any landing page                    |

AIDA is usually credited to E. St. Elmo Lewis around 1898, though the attribution is debated. The "4Ps" circulates in two versions (Promise, Picture, Proof, Push; and Ray Edwards' Problem, Promise, Proof, Proposal), so say which one you mean.

## Examples (illustrative)

PAS:

> Spreadsheets lose track of who owes you. Every missed follow-up is rent money sitting in someone else's account. Paydue sends the reminders for you.

BAB:

> Before: 40 unpaid invoices and a Friday spent chasing. After: paid on time, Fridays free. The bridge: automatic reminders.

FAB:

> 256-bit encryption (feature), the same protection banks use (advantage), so you pass your client's security review (benefit).

## Bad

Picking PAS first, then inventing a problem to agitate. Or forcing every section of a homepage into AIDA when visitors arrive at different stages.

## Fix

Do the research. List the points and objections. Then pick the framework whose order matches your reader's awareness stage (Rule 03): PAS or BAB for problem-aware, FAB for product pages, offer-first for most-aware.
