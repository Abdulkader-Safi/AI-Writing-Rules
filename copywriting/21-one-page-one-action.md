# Rule 21: One page, one main action

## What it is

Each landing page should ask for one thing. Other links and buttons compete for the same click. A homepage can route several audiences, but a landing page built for a campaign or offer should have one primary CTA.

## Why it works

Julian Shapiro's formula: purchase rate = desire - (labour + confusion). Every extra choice adds confusion. A page with "Book a demo", "Start trial", "Read the blog" and "Follow us" asks the visitor to decide what to decide.

The live Copyhackers sales page (January 2026) repeats the same main CTA three times and nothing else competes with it.

## Check

- Count the distinct actions the page asks for. Landing page target: one primary, at most one secondary (for example "Book a call" plus "See pricing").
- Is the main navigation needed on this page, or does it lead people away?
- Does every repeated CTA use the same wording?

## Bad

A Google Ads landing page with full site navigation, a newsletter pop-up, a chat widget, three different buttons ("Get a quote", "Call now", "Download brochure") and social links in the hero.

## Good

Hero: headline, subhead, one button "Get my quote". The same button repeats after the benefits, after the testimonials and at the end. A phone number in the header as the one alternative for people who prefer to call.

## Fix

Decide the one action before writing. Remove or tone down everything that competes with it. Make secondary options text links, not buttons.

Source: [Julian Shapiro: landing pages](https://www.julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages), [Copyhackers sales page (2026)](https://copyhackers.com/ultimate-guide-landing-page-copy)

Evidence: practitioner guidance. No controlled test found in this research.
