# Rule 23: Landing page: one offer, objections answered in order

## What it is

A landing page is built for one offer and one audience, usually the destination of an ad, email or campaign. Its structure follows the visitor's questions in the order they arise.

## Two proven orders

Julian Shapiro (proof first, for visitors who already want this kind of product):

1. Navbar
2. Hero: header (what it is), subheader (why care, 10 to 13 words), image, CTA
3. Social proof (logos, numbers)
4. CTA
5. 3 to 6 feature blocks, each with a blunt header, a paragraph that answers a real objection and an image of the product working
6. Repeated CTA
7. Footer

Copyhackers sales page, January 2026 (problem first, for visitors who still need convincing):

1. Hero
2. The problem, then a reframe of it
3. The offer
4. Founder credentials
5. Testimonials with specific, dated results
6. Closing CTA that restates the headline
7. Refund policy

The main CTA appears three times. Matching these orders to awareness stages is an interpretation, not something either source states.

## Why it works

Shapiro's formula: purchase rate = desire - (labour + confusion). Each section either adds desire or removes labour and confusion. Sections that do neither get cut.

## Bad

A landing page that copies the homepage: full navigation, every service listed, a generic "Contact us" button, no answers to the questions this specific ad raised.

## Good

For an ad "Website redesign for Kuwait clinics":

> Hero: "Clinic websites that fill your appointment book." / "Online booking in Arabic and English, live in 6 weeks." / [Get a fixed quote]
> Proof: "60 clinics in Kuwait and Saudi Arabia"
> Blocks: "Patients book without calling" / "Ranks for 'dentist in Salmiya'" / "You update it yourself" / "Fixed price, no surprise invoices"
> Testimonial next to the price block
> FAQ: timeline, cost, who writes the content, what happens after launch
> [Get a fixed quote] / "Reply within one business day"

(Illustrative.)

## Fix

Write down the questions the ad's audience will have, in order. Give each a section. Put risk reversal (Rule 14) near the final CTA. Remove the main navigation if it leads away from the offer.

Source: [Julian Shapiro: landing pages](https://www.julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages), [Copyhackers sales page (2026)](https://copyhackers.com/ultimate-guide-landing-page-copy)

Evidence: practitioner guidance and one live example. No comparative test of section orders.
