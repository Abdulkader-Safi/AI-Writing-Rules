# Rule 24: Product and service pages: highlights, not feature dumps

## What it is

Structure product and service descriptions around 2 to 6 core benefits ("highlights"), each with an image, a short headline and one paragraph. Put secondary features in a plain bullet list below.

## Why it works

In Baymard's product-page testing, every user relied on the description at some point to judge whether the product fit. Yet only 22% of e-commerce sites structured descriptions by highlights. Most sites miss this.

Findings:

- Plain bullet lists became feature dumps that users skipped.
- Highlight-structured descriptions were explored more thoroughly and left users more positive about the product.
- Icons did worse than real images but still beat text-only descriptions.

This is 2018 research on e-commerce. Applying it to service and software pages is a reasonable inference that Baymard didn't test.

## Structure

1. Name and one-line summary of what it is and who it's for
2. 2 to 6 highlights: image + short headline + one paragraph each
3. "Also included": plain bullets for secondary features
4. Specs or technical details for those who want them
5. Proof and CTA

Use FAB (feature, advantage, benefit) to turn each spec into a benefit the reader cares about.

## Bad

> Features: SSO, audit logs, role-based access, API, webhooks, 99.9% uptime, GDPR, export, custom fields, integrations.

## Good

> **Pass your client's security review**
> Single sign-on, audit logs and role-based access come standard on every plan. Send your client's IT team our security sheet and skip the back-and-forth.
>
> **Your data, your way**
> Export everything as CSV or connect through the API. No lock-in.
>
> Also included: webhooks, custom fields, 99.9% uptime SLA.

(Illustrative.)

## Fix

Group your feature list into the few outcomes the customer cares about. Each outcome becomes a highlight with a benefit headline. Everything that doesn't fit goes in the bullet list.

Source: [Baymard: structure descriptions by highlights (2018)](https://baymard.com/research-articles/structure-descriptions-by-highlights)

Evidence: strong but old for e-commerce (Baymard, 2018). Inference for services.
