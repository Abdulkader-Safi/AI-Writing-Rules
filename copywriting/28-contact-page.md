# Rule 28: Contact page: say what happens next

## What it is

The contact page should make getting in touch easy and remove the worry about what happens after. Several channels, a short form and a line saying who replies and how fast.

## Why it works

NN/g's About Us research (2019): offer several easy-to-find contact channels (phone, email, address, chat). Easy contact options raised user satisfaction.

NN/g's form guidance (2016): keep the form short, mark the one or two optional fields, put labels above fields, give format hints up front, keep the user's input when there's an error, and use one primary button with no Reset button.

Saying who will reply and how fast is an inference. It answers the obvious question at the point of action, which is what the checkout evidence rewards (Rule 14).

## Structure

1. A heading that invites the right enquiry
2. One line: who replies, how fast, what happens next
3. A short form (name, email, message, maybe one qualifying question)
4. Other channels: phone, WhatsApp, email, address with map, office hours

## Bad

> Contact us
> Fill out the form below.
> Name* Email* Phone* Company* Job title* Budget* How did you hear about us?* Message*
> [Submit] [Reset]

## Good

> Tell us about your project
> A senior engineer replies within one business day with next steps.
> Name / Email / What do you need built? / Budget range (optional)
> [Send my enquiry]
> Prefer to talk? Call or WhatsApp +965 ..., Sunday to Thursday, 9 to 5.

(Illustrative.)

## Fix

Cut every field you don't need for the first reply. State the response time you can keep. Rename the button with the outcome (Rule 13). Add at least one channel besides the form.

Source: [NN/g: About Us information (2019)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/about-us-information-on-websites/), [NN/g: web form design (2016)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/)

Evidence: strong but old (NN/g 2016, 2019). Response-time line is an inference.
