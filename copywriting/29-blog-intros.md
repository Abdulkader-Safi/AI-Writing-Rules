# Rule 29: Blog posts: the answer first

## What it is

Start a blog post or article with the answer, or with the reader's problem and the promise of the answer. Background and detail come after, in falling order of importance.

## Why it works

The inverted pyramid puts the conclusion first, so readers who stop early still get the point (NN/g, 2018). Given that people read about 20% of a page (Rule 06), most of them do stop early.

Ahrefs (2023) recommends an intro of about two paragraphs using problem, solution, proof, and matching the format that already ranks for the keyword (a list, a guide, a comparison). Google has no preferred word count.

AI answers raise the stakes further (Rule 32). The first sentence under each heading may be all that a person or a model reads.

## Check

- Does the first paragraph answer the title's question, or say clearly what the post will give?
- Could the intro be deleted without losing information? If yes, it's throat-clearing.

## Bad

> In today's fast-paced digital world, website speed has become more important than ever. Many businesses wonder whether their site is fast enough. In this article, we will explore the topic of website speed and why it matters.

## Good

> A page that takes more than a few seconds to load loses visitors before they see anything. Here are the four fixes that make the biggest difference, in order, and how to check each one in five minutes.

## Fix

Delete the first paragraph of your draft and see if the post still works. It usually does. Rewrite the new opening to state the answer or the payoff. Put the main point of each section in its first sentence.

Source: [NN/g: inverted pyramid (2018)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/inverted-pyramid/), [Ahrefs: how to format a blog post (2023)](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-to-format-a-blog-post/), [Google: creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

Evidence: strong for the principle (NN/g); practitioner for the intro formula (Ahrefs).
