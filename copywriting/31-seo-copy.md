# Rule 31: Write search titles and headings for people first

## What it is

SEO copy is the text search engines show and read: the page title, the meta description, headings and link text. Google's guidance is to write it for people, with the words people actually search for.

## Why it works

Google's helpful-content guidance asks whether a page:

- offers original information
- shows who wrote it and why they know the subject
- would be worth bookmarking or sharing

Warning signs include writing for search traffic, summarising others without adding anything, changing dates without real updates and writing to a target word count. Google has no preferred length. Trust is the most important part of E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trust). AI-assisted content is allowed, but mass-produced pages made to rank count as scaled content abuse.

GOV.UK adds: use the words people search for. Plain language and SEO point the same way.

## Rules

Page title:

- Unique per page, descriptive, concise.
- Important words first, brand at the start or end.
- Match the main visible headline.
- Google sets no character limit but truncates to device width. The common 50 to 60 character guide is industry convention, not Google guidance.

Meta description:

- Summarise this specific page with useful facts (price, date, location).
- No keyword lists. Google often writes its own snippet from page content anyway.

Headings and links:

- A real H1, H2, H3 structure.
- Plain-text internal links between related pages.

## Bad

```html
<title>Home</title>
<meta
  name="description"
  content="web design, web development, SEO, Kuwait, best web design company, cheap websites"
/>
```

## Good

```html
<title>Clinic booking and payment software | Brightline</title>
<meta
  name="description"
  content="Online booking in Arabic and English for clinics in Kuwait and Australia. Patients book in under a minute. From KWD 25/month."
/>
```

(Illustrative.)

## Fix

Find the phrase your customers search for (Search Console, keyword tools, your review research). Put it near the start of the title and in the H1, worded naturally. Write the meta description as an advert for that one page.

Evidence: strong (Google's own documentation).
