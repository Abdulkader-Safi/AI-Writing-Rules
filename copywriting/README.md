# Copywriting: rules for website copy

36 rules, one per file. They cover conversion copy (getting a sign-up, sale, lead or booking) and general website copy (clear, scannable, on-brand, accessible, findable). Each file has what the rule is, why it works, a check, a bad example, a good example, the fix and its sources.

These sit alongside the [AI writing patterns](../research/README.md). The patterns stop copy sounding machine-made. These rules make it do its job.

## The core finding

Copy that converts and copy that is easy to use are mostly the same copy. Independent usability research (NN/g, Baymard, GOV.UK) and the conversion case studies reward the same moves: say the plain thing first, answer the real question, prove it with something specific. Persuasion techniques only work on top of that.

## How strong is the evidence?

Every file ends with an evidence label.

| Label      | Meaning                                                              |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strong     | Independent research with a stated method, or official guidance      |
| Old        | Data from before 2020. Often reconfirmed, but check the date         |
| Vendor     | Published by a company that sells the tool or service                |
| Anecdote   | A single test with no sample size, duration or significance reported |
| Convention | Widely taught, with no test evidence found                           |

Most famous copy tests are 2009 to 2013, changed several things at once and reported no confidence level. Treat every single-test percentage as an example of a principle, not a number to expect.

## Measurable thresholds, collected

| Rule               | Metric                                             | Target                          |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Headline           | Stranger can say what you sell after 5 seconds     | Yes                             |
| Subhead            | Length                                             | 10 to 13 words                  |
| Reading level      | Grade (Flesch-Kincaid, Hemingway)                  | 5 to 7 for most pages           |
| Sentences          | Average / maximum length                           | 15 to 20 / about 25 words       |
| Paragraphs         | Sentences / words                                  | 5 or fewer / under 150          |
| Product highlights | Core features with image + headline + paragraph    | 2 to 6                          |
| Forms              | Optional fields                                    | 1 to 2 at most, clearly marked  |
| CTA label          | Length, starts with a verb                         | 2 to 5 words                    |
| Landing page       | Primary actions                                    | 1                               |
| A/B test           | Conversions per variation before trusting a result | 250 to 350+                     |
| A/B test           | Duration                                           | Whole weeks, 2+ business cycles |

## Gaps in the evidence

- No primary research on pricing-page copy was found.
- Case-study structure and FAQ placement are convention.
- No study compares "you"-focused and "we"-focused copy.
- No controlled test compares AI-written and human-written website copy.
- The legal rules covered are US (FTC). Australian and GCC consumer law was not researched.
